“You know what created value last year.

And which three things should you fix first?

Most companies can list what created value — revenue growth, cost control, a handful of initiatives, a new platform. The list is not the problem. The ordering is. Ranking requires attribution, and attribution is the one thing a list does not carry.

AXIOM answers that question with a document, not an assertion. The ranking is the product’s output — a decomposition of the change in what the business is worth, driver by driver, with the part nothing explains left visible.

Your actuals are one input of four, and they are the one you already have. The other three — initiative status, stakeholder signal, and a valuation model — were never general-ledger objects. That is not a gap in the finance function’s work. Those things live in different systems, or in nobody’s.

First, what AXIOM does not do.

It does not touch your ledger.

It does not close your books.

It does not replace the CFO’s pack.

The management accounts answer what happened. AXIOM answers what it is worth and what to do next. Those are different questions, and the second pair cannot be derived from the ledger — the inputs are not in it.

AXIOM is not an extension of your reporting stack. A product that extends an existing pack is a line item on top of a line item, and the first budget review removes it.

In the same demo pack, two of six drivers are computable and four are declared absent by name — trading, forecast revision, discount rate and multiples — each with the reason it could not be computed. AXIOM reports what it cannot compute rather than filling the gap.

The Decision Record

Decisions taken against a pack are recorded with who took them and when, and the following pack reports what followed. This is what closes the loop — the pack that shows whether the last pack’s decision was worth taking.

The Watch — event-timed, not monthly

Some things cannot wait for the pack. A covenant breach on the 12th reported on the 5th is a post-mortem, not a warning. The Watch alerts named recipients when a threshold is crossed, on its own timing, and appears in the next pack as part of what is at risk.

The second question

“How would you know?”

A ranked decomposition is worth having only if the ranking is defensible. This page is for the reader who asks; it is skippable by the reader who does not.

What distinguishes AXIOM is not that the mathematics is more advanced. It is that a corporate-finance practitioner set the rules. Each discipline below is a decision you would recognise as one only someone who has done valuation work would make — and each is visible in the artefact, so you can check it rather than take it.

The clearest example is the registry declining to pin itself

AXIOM holds a registry of ratio definitions that production does not yet read. Rather than pin a version for it, the pack records that it is not consumed, and why — because pinning a version for something nothing renders would assert more than the system knows.

One definition per number — stated as it actually works

Six quantities — net debt, total debt, invested capital, return on invested capital, economic value added and cost of capital — have a declared owner and an automated check that fails the build when the number of places computing them changes.

Five of the six resolve to a single site today. Total debt is computed in seventeen places, and the check holds that number so it cannot grow unnoticed. We would rather tell you the number than claim it is one.

Two documents, deliberately different

The pack is selective — seven sections, so a reader can navigate it. The export is exhaustive — every section AXIOM can produce, for readers who do not have access to the application and should not have to guess what was left out. An export that fails by being incomplete is a different failure from a pack that fails by being long, and they are built to different rules.

What the loop contains

Analyse

Financial performance and ratios; enterprise valuation on two methods with their disagreement stated; a solvency boundary and the distance from today’s position to it; benchmarking; and a structured assessment of the organisation from the people inside it.

Strategise

Forecasting on five methods, with the spread reported when they disagree rather than averaged away. A joint search across margin, working capital, capital intensity, growth and leverage under solvency constraints, returning each lever’s marginal contribution. Flexibility — the option to expand, defer or abandon — priced rather than asserted.

Execute

Objectives, key results and initiatives with declared ownership; initiatives linked to the statement lines they are declared to move, at a declared share; and the decision record that reports what followed.

What it is for, in order of what it is worth.

1 · Value-maximising actions

What to do next, ranked by what it is worth doing — the joint lever search, with each lever’s marginal contribution and the constraints that bind.

2 · Risk, volatility and underperformance

Where solvency breaks, how far today’s position is from that edge, and which measures are moving the wrong way — monitored between cycles rather than discovered at the next one.

3 · Stakeholder feedback — the causal chain

This is not a softer fourth feature. It is what stops the headline number being a claim. A signal from the organisation reaches an initiative, the initiative owns a key result, the key result drives a measure, and the measure moves a line in the statements. Each hop is a record you can open.

Responses are anonymous, and the anonymity survives arithmetic: results are suppressed below a minimum respondent count, suppression extends to the complement so a small group cannot be recovered by subtraction, and the floor is applied to the department-and-seniority cell rather than to either dimension alone.

4 · Alignment

Objectives, key results, measures and initiatives in one line of sight, so that the thing being reported and the thing being worked on are the same thing.
