# §8 — FREE PILOT & PARTNER PROGRAM
## Commercial surface · draft for founder ruling · 31 July 2026

Not locked until it is in the ledger. Brochure v2 page 9 sells a seven-step
Free Pilot and two partner tiers with specific sums attached. Neither has a
system behind it.

**Exposure:** the brochure states $500 to join as a Referral Partner, ten per
cent of first-year subscription revenue, a ten per cent client discount, $4,500
for Certified Implementation Partner, and a twenty-five per cent advisory
royalty. Those are offers. Today there is no signup, no attribution, no
tracking, and no payout path. **A partner who joins on those terms and
introduces a client has no way to be credited, and no way to prove they should
have been.**

---

## PART A — FREE PILOT

### A.1 Lifecycle

Seven public-facing steps, seven internal states, one-to-one. A super-admin
**Pilot Companies** tab is the system of record.

| State | Entered when | Exit |
|---|---|---|
| Requested | `/free-pilot` form submitted | scoping call held |
| Scoped | entity and cycle agreed | data received |
| Loading | statements, plan, org received | ingest complete |
| Calibrated | model computes clean | assessment invites sent |
| Assessing | cycle open | cycle closed |
| Reports ready | reports generated | live review held |
| Reviewed | walkthrough complete | **decision point** |

From Reviewed, three terminal states: **Transferred** (purchased),
**Lapsed** (no decision within window), **Declined**.

**Lapse window ruling needed.** Registry default: **60 days from Reviewed**,
then Lapsed. Without a window, Pilot Companies becomes an unbounded list of
maybes and the tab stops being a pipeline.

### A.2 What happens at Lapsed — the ruling that matters

The pilot runs the **full engine including Prescience** on the client's real
financials, real org structure, and real assessment responses from their named
executives. That is not a demo dataset. It is their company.

**Registry default: on Lapsed, the workspace is frozen, not deleted.** Read
access revoked, data retained 12 months, restorable on purchase within that
window. Deletion available on written request at any time.

**Reasoning.** Deleting on lapse destroys assessment responses given by
executives under a participation guarantee, and destroys the client's own
uploaded files, of which AXIOM may hold the only structured version. Retaining
indefinitely is a data-protection position nobody wants to defend. Twelve months
with restore-on-purchase matches the natural re-approach cycle.

**This needs saying on `/free-pilot` before the form**, not in a footer. A CFO
handing over statements and letting AXIOM survey their leadership will ask, and
the answer being ready is worth more than the answer being generous.

### A.3 The Prescience problem

The pilot runs Prescience. Most buyers will buy **Business** at $4,995.

**Conversion is therefore a downgrade**, and the client will notice which
features left: Ask AXIOM, decision search, executive priorities alignment, the
dedicated CSM, plus the seat caps tightening from 25/15/150 to 10/5/50.

Three options:

**(a) Show what leaves, before purchase.** At the review step, a comparison of
what the pilot ran versus what each edition retains. Honest, and makes the
$7,000 upgrade a considered decision rather than a discovery.

**(b) Run the pilot on Business.** Removes the problem, weakens the pilot, and
contradicts the brochure's "nothing is watered down."

**(c) Grace period.** Prescience retained 30 days post-purchase, then reverts
unless upgraded.

**Registry default: (a).** (c) reads as a trial-within-a-trial and makes the
reversion date the memorable event. (b) gives up the strongest thing about the
pilot motion.

### A.4 Seat handling at transfer

Pilot assessors responded under a participation invitation, not an account.
On transfer, **their responses transfer; their access does not** — consistent
with assessors never holding strategy-surface access. The buying entity assigns
member and viewer seats fresh against the purchased caps.

## PART B — PARTNER PROGRAM

### B.1 The structural ruling — who is the EID?

Everything else depends on this. The two-tier EID/CID model already recognises a
**consulting firm** EID segment holding many CIDs. A Tier 2 partner could be
either:

**(a) Partner is the EID.** Partner holds the contract, pays AXIOM, and their
clients are CIDs inside the partner's account. Partner sets client pricing.
AXIOM's customer is the partner.

**(b) Client is the EID.** Client contracts with AXIOM directly and pays AXIOM.
Partner is credited and paid commission or royalty. AXIOM's customer is the
client.

**These are different businesses, not different settings.** Under (a) AXIOM
never has a relationship with the end client, cannot see churn coming, and
cannot upsell. Under (b) the partner is an introducer and a service layer, and
the client can dismiss them without losing the platform.

**Registry default: (b) for both tiers, with (a) available as a negotiated
enterprise arrangement for firms bringing a book.** Reasoning: (b) preserves the
direct relationship, keeps pricing integrity — a partner reselling under (a) will
discount to win and the $4,995 floor erodes invisibly — and makes the ten per
cent client discount a controlled instrument rather than the opening bid in a
negotiation AXIOM cannot see.

Under (b), a Tier 2 partner running pilots and quarterly cycles for a client
holds **delegated access to that client's workspace, granted by the client and
revocable by the client.** Not ownership.

### B.2 Attribution

The commercial claim is ten per cent of first-year subscription revenue. That
requires knowing which partner brought which client, defensibly.

- **Partner code**, issued at joining, carried on `/free-pilot` and on any
  direct enquiry form.
- **First touch wins**, recorded at Requested. A client arriving with no code and
  later claimed by a partner is **not** retroactively attributed — that is the
  dispute the whole mechanism exists to prevent.
- **Attribution window: 180 days** from Requested to first paid invoice. Pilots
  legitimately run long; a 90-day window would strand honest introductions.
- Attribution is **visible to the partner from Requested onward**, so a missing
  code surfaces immediately rather than at invoice.

### B.3 Commission mechanics — four gaps in the published terms

**1. Commission base — gross or net of the client discount?**
The client gets ten per cent off; the partner gets ten per cent commission.
Registry default: **commission on the discounted amount actually invoiced.**
On Business at $4,995, discount $499.50, invoice $4,495.50, commission $449.55.
Paying commission on the undiscounted list price means AXIOM funds both sides of
the same ten per cent, which is a 20% effective cost of acquisition on a
recurring product.

**2. "First-year" — from when?**
Registry default: **twelve months from first paid invoice**, not from pilot
start or signup.

**3. Upgrades mid-year.**
A client on Business who takes the $7,000 Prescience upgrade in month four.
Registry default: **commission follows the invoice**, so the partner earns on
the upgraded amount for the remainder of the twelve months. It rewards the
behaviour AXIOM wants and preserves zero arbitrage — the partner cannot earn
more by structuring the sale differently.

**4. Churn and clawback.**
Registry default: **commission paid monthly in arrears on collected revenue.**
No clawback provision needed, because nothing is paid on revenue not received.
Simpler than pay-upfront-and-reclaim, and avoids invoicing a partner for money
already spent.

### B.4 Tier 2 — two undefined terms

**"$4,500, payable after your first booked client."**
Registry default: **booked = first paid invoice from a client attributed to that
partner**, not pilot start and not signature. Payable within 30 days of that
invoice clearing. A partner who never books never pays, which is what the
brochure implies and should therefore be true.

**"Twenty-five per cent royalty on advisory revenue."**
Ambiguous in the published copy, and the two readings differ by an order of
magnitude:

- **(i)** Partner delivers advisory to their client; AXIOM takes 25%.
- **(ii)** AXIOM delivers DCT Advisory to the partner's client; partner takes 25%.

Brochure context — "run pilots and quarterly cycles yourself" — points to **(i)**,
the partner delivering and AXIOM taking a royalty on the methodology. But the
word *royalty* alongside a partner benefits list reads as money flowing **to**
the partner, which is (ii).

**This needs your ruling and the brochure line needs rewording either way.** It
is the one place in the published document where a reader could reasonably form
the opposite belief to the one intended.

### B.5 Isolation

A partner sees **only** their own attributed clients — never another partner's,
never a direct client. Enforced at the server, consistent with page-level access
control being enforced server-side rather than hidden in the interface.

Partner-facing surface shows: attributed clients and lifecycle state, commission
accrued and paid, and nothing about the client's financials unless the client has
granted delegated workspace access separately. **Attribution is not access.**

## ROUTING

**→ CLAUDE CODE.** Money, attribution and isolation are all server-side
contracts. Partner code issuance, first-touch capture, the 180-day window,
commission on collected revenue, and cross-partner isolation cannot be
UI-enforced.

**→ LOVABLE** for the `/free-pilot` public page — greenfield, visual, brass
seven-step stepper, no contract behind it beyond a form POST. The form's
attribution capture is a contract and stays in the Claude Code lane.

**Sequencing:** independent of §7r, §7m and §7s. Can run whenever.
