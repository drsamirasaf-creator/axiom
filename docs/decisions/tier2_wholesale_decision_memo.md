## 31 July 2026 · the last item gating the partner motion

The brochure now tells a Tier 2 partner they can hold the account and bill their
own client. That is only true once there is a number for what they pay AXIOM.
This models the choice.

**Anchor:** AXIOM Business, $4,995/month, $59,940/year.

## 3. THE FINDING THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE RATE

**Any wholesale discount breaks zero arbitrage across the channel**, and it
breaks it in the direction that is hardest to see.

| Route | Client actually pays /mo |
|---|---:|
| Direct from AXIOM | 4,995.00 |
| Via Tier 1 referral | 4,495.50 |
| Via Tier 2 partner @ 20%, selling thin | 4,245.75 |
| Via Tier 2 partner @ 30%, selling thin | 3,746.25 |

A Tier 2 partner buying at 20% can undercut your own referral price and still
make $250/month. At 30% they can undercut it by 25% and make a living doing it.

**The consequence is not lost margin — you already agreed to the wholesale rate.
It is that the wholesale rate becomes the real list price**, discovered by
clients who talk to each other, and AXIOM has no visibility of it because the
partner's invoice is not in your system.

This is the same discipline as the DLOM and the two-ROICs problem, in commercial
form: **one quantity, one owner.** A price that means four different things
depending on route is not a price.

### Three ways to hold it

**(a) Tier 2 at list — 0% wholesale.**
Partner's entire margin is advisory, where they keep 75%. The floor holds
absolutely and there is no channel conflict, because there is no channel margin.

This makes Tier 2 **an advisory licence with account-holding convenience, not a
reseller model.** The EID structure then serves the partner's workflow — one
workspace, whole book, transfer on demand — rather than their economics. That may
be exactly what you want, but it should be chosen knowingly, and the $4,500 then
buys certification and the right to bill advisory, not a margin.

**(b) Wholesale with a floor price covenant.**
20% off, contractually bound to a minimum client-facing price of $4,495.50 — the
Tier 1 referral price. Partner keeps up to $999/month, cannot undercut your own
channel, and the $4,995 list stays true everywhere. Unenforceable without an
audit right, but so is the advisory royalty, and the covenant makes the
expectation explicit rather than hoping.

**(c) Volume tiers.**
Wholesale improves with CID count — 10% at 1–4, 20% at 5–14, 25% at 15+.
Rewards book-building, keeps the entry rate close to list so a one-client
partner cannot undercut, and gives a partner a reason to grow rather than to
discount. Standard shape for this model, and the most work to administer.

---

## RECOMMENDATION

**(c), with (b)'s floor covenant attached.**

Volume tiers because the risk is concentrated in small partners — one client, a
deep discount, and a price on the street that undercuts you. Starting them near
list removes that. The covenant because it costs nothing to state and converts
an unenforceable hope into a term you can point at.

**If you want this simple rather than right, (a) is the honest simple answer** —
list price, advisory-only margin, no channel conflict possible. It is a smaller
business than (c) but it has no failure mode.
