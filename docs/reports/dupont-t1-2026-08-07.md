### ⭐⭐ It is wholly a basis artefact, and that is proven rather than asserted

Recomputing `asset_turnover` and `financial_leverage` **point-in-time** — same
data, same periods, only the basis changed:

| year | dupont (point-in-time) | roe | residual |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 18.1425 | 18.1425 | **0.000000** |
| 2023 | 17.9758 | 17.9758 | **0.000000** |
| 2024 | 17.8122 | 17.8122 | **0.000000** |
| 2025 | 17.6151 | 17.6151 | **0.000000** |

⭐ **Exactly zero in every period.** The identity
`net_margin × asset_turnover × financial_leverage = ROE` holds *identically* on a
consistent basis. **There is no arithmetic defect. The entire residual is the
basis mix**, and the average factors' `avg()` is what displaces it.

⛔ **The ruling is therefore narrower than "how do we reconcile":** it is *which
basis DuPont is on*. Three options, none taken here:

1. move the two average factors to `point_in_time` — the identity then holds
   exactly and `display_rule` never fires;
2. keep the mixed basis and render the residual, which is what `display_rule`
   already requires;
3. restate `display_rule` to reconcile against an **average-basis ROE**, which
   does not currently exist in the registry.
